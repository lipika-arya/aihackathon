# AbroadROI: Product & MVP Documentation

**User journeys · Research approach · AI-assisted development · Technology stack**  
**Version:** 22 October 2026  
**Live MVP:** https://aihackathon-three.vercel.app/  
**Repository:** https://github.com/lipika-arya/aihackathon

## 1. Executive summary

AbroadROI is a decision-support web app for Indian students and families weighing a master's degree abroad against the financial alternative of staying in India. It brings programme costs, education-loan repayment, salary assumptions, visa/work-permit risk and downside scenarios into one place.

The product treats overseas education as a major financial decision, not only a university-ranking choice. Students can examine potential upside and consider what happens if a job search takes longer, work authorization ends, or they return to India.

| Item | Current MVP |
|---|---|
| Audience | Indian students considering postgraduate study and the family members helping fund it |
| Destinations | Canada, Germany, United States, United Kingdom, Japan and Australia |
| Study tracks | MS in Computer Science, Master's in Data Science and MBA |
| Core features | Assessment, programme explorer, cost and funding-gap calculations, loan/EMI modelling, ROI scenarios, visa information and methodology |
| Deployment | Vercel |
| Research data | Curated, version-controlled JSON with source links and evidence-status labels |
| AI approach | Claude Code was used as the coding assistant. Financial calculations and recommendations are deterministic, not generated by an LLM |

## 2. Problem and product principles

Students can find rankings, tuition pages, salary estimates and loan calculators in different places, but it is difficult to combine them into one personal decision. A headline salary does not show the effect of unemployment, borrowing costs, or a return to India.

AbroadROI is guided by these principles:

- Compare the full cost, not tuition alone.
- Show funding gaps and loan repayments separately from educational return.
- Make assumptions visible and editable.
- Compare studying abroad with a user-entered India salary baseline where available.
- Show multiple possible futures rather than imply one outcome is guaranteed.
- Label data by evidence status and link to sources.
- Treat unknown information as unknown instead of silently converting it to zero.

## 3. Current MVP scope

The original brief began with five destinations and three course tracks. During implementation, the curated MVP expanded to include the United Kingdom as a sixth destination. India is used as a comparison baseline in financial scenarios rather than as another overseas destination.

**Included in the MVP**
- Student assessment and profile inputs.
- University/programme explorer and comparisons.
- Total cost, funding gap, loan terms and EMI estimates.
- Five scenarios: fast job, expected job, slow search, work permit ends/return home, and return home after graduation.
- Visa/work-policy information and an in-app methodology page.
- Currency conversion using Frankfurter/ECB reference rates with a stored fallback.
- Browser-local profile persistence, without requiring an account.

**Not claimed as complete**
- Exhaustive coverage of every university/programme.
- Guaranteed admission, employment, visa approval or permanent residency.
- AI-generated probabilities for job, visa or immigration outcomes.
- Official government-issued visa-risk ratings.
- A full production data platform, institutional dashboard, payment system or cloud-saved profile.

## 4. User journeys

### Journey A: First-time student assessment
1. The student lands on the homepage and understands the promise: evaluate cost and risk before committing to a large expense.
2. They open the assessment and enter academic details, course interests, target countries and career goals.
3. They enter available funds, savings, scholarship, maximum loan and repayment preferences. To compare ROI against India, they can provide their own India salary assumption.
4. They review assumptions such as lifestyle, risk tolerance, repayment period and financial priorities.
5. AbroadROI calculates programme costs, financing needs and scenario results.
6. The student reviews comparisons and changes inputs to see how the outcome changes.

### Journey B: Explore and compare programmes
1. Browse or filter the curated university/programme set.
2. Open a programme to review location, duration, tuition basis, cost assumptions, requirements and source links.
3. Compare estimated total cost and affordability with other options.
4. Check evidence labels and missing-fee warnings before treating an estimate as decision-ready.

### Journey C: Understand the loan and funding gap
1. Enter family funds, savings, scholarship and the maximum loan the student is willing to take.
2. Review total funding requirement, including applicable adjustments and the loan processing fee.
3. See how much is covered by own funds and borrowing, and how much remains unfunded.
4. Review estimated EMI, interest and repayment timeline under the chosen loan assumptions.

### Journey D: Stress-test the decision

| Scenario | Question it helps answer |
|---|---|
| Job abroad fast | What if the student finds work soon after graduation? |
| Expected job abroad | What happens under the model's central salary and job-search assumptions? |
| Slow job search | Can the student absorb a longer period without a full-time job? |
| Work permit ends / return to India | What if the student cannot remain employed abroad and returns home? |
| Return home after graduation | Can the loan be serviced using the India income assumption? |

These are scenario analyses, not predictions or assigned probabilities. Results depend on salary, job-search delay, exchange-rate movement, tuition growth, living costs and loan terms.

### Journey E: Parent/family review
1. Review total cost and the amount that must be funded before admission or loan commitment.
2. Focus on the downside case, EMI burden and the return-to-India scenario.
3. Open methodology and source links to understand which values are verified, estimated or user-entered.
4. List remaining questions to confirm with the university, lender or official immigration authority.

### Journey F: Missing or stale data
When a source cannot confirm a material figure, the data should be labelled partial/incomplete or omitted if it cannot responsibly be shown. If live exchange-rate retrieval fails, the app can use a stored fallback rate and label it not current. A missing mandatory fee must not be interpreted as proof that the fee is zero.

## 5. Research approach and evidence

The MVP uses a curated dataset rather than attempting to crawl every university. The project brief prioritised traceable information and prohibited fabricated fees, salary figures, exchange rates or policy claims.

| Research area | Preferred evidence | Use in the product |
|---|---|---|
| University/programme costs | Official university programme, tuition and international-student pages | Tuition, duration, fees, requirements and source links |
| Visa and work rights | Official government/immigration sources where accessible | Visa costs, proof-of-funds, work rights and post-study work rules |
| Salary/employment context | Official labour-market or graduate-outcome sources where available | Entry-level salary assumptions and scenario context |
| Education loans | Lender's own published rates and terms | Interest-rate ranges, moratorium assumptions, repayment terms and fees |
| Currency conversion | Frankfurter API using ECB reference rates | Convert foreign-currency amounts to INR; cache daily with a labelled fallback |
| India comparison | Student's own India salary input | Avoid inventing an unsupported salary benchmark; ROI comparisons become available when the user supplies a baseline |

### Evidence labels
- **Verified:** checked against the cited source for the stated use.
- **Partial:** some elements are supported, but one or more details remain unclear or incomplete.
- **Estimate/assumption:** a model input or projection, not a guaranteed fact.
- **User input:** supplied by the student and used to personalise calculations.

The repository's data audit is dated **22 October 2026**. It records a review of approximately 470 fields across country datasets and lender data, lists corrections made, and documents data removed because it could not be confirmed or was not current. It also identifies remaining partial items and incomplete mandatory-fee estimates. This is an audit record, not a guarantee that every source will remain current.

### Research limitations
- Some official pages block automated reading or publish fees in dynamic tables; such cases are documented rather than treated as fully verified.
- The programme catalogue is curated, not exhaustive.
- Salary sources may cover broader experience bands or use proxies; limitations are noted where applicable.
- Tax is modelled using a simplified effective rate, not a full personal tax calculation.
- Visa policy risk is AbroadROI's comparative index, not an official statistic or an individual's visa-approval probability.
- Policy and lender information can change; high-impact details should be rechecked before a real decision.

## 6. How the calculations work

The application uses deterministic TypeScript functions. Given the same inputs and data, calculations produce the same output; an LLM does not invent or calculate the financial figures.

| Metric | Plain-language explanation |
|---|---|
| Total cost | Combines tuition and available cost components such as living expenses, travel/application items and applicable fees or adjustments. |
| Funding gap | The amount the student's own funds and planned loan cannot cover, including the applicable processing fee in the financing model. |
| EMI | Calculated from loan principal, annual interest rate and repayment duration using the standard amortising-loan formula. |
| Loan simulation | Models costs and borrowing month by month, including a moratorium/grace period, interest handling and repayment. |
| Economic ROI | Compares incremental financial cash flows from the overseas-study path with the India baseline over the chosen horizon. |
| NPV and IRR | Discounted cash-flow metrics from modelled cash flows; IRR is unavailable when it is not meaningful. |
| Visa policy risk | A weighted comparative index using policy volatility, post-study-work uncertainty, work restrictions, proof-of-funds burden and recent adverse changes. |
| Recommendation score | A transparent rule-based weighted score. Risk is reversed so higher risk reduces suitability. If India salary is missing, the ROI component is excluded rather than guessed. |

Key assumptions include job-search delay, salary level/growth, INR depreciation, tuition growth, living-cost inflation, loan interest, moratorium, processing fee and discount rate. These assumptions can materially change results, so the output is a decision aid rather than a promise of future earnings.

## 7. How AI was used

### Claude Code as the primary development tool
Claude Code was used throughout implementation as the AI coding assistant. It supported translating the product brief into application code, iterating on interface and feature flows, implementing and refining calculation modules, addressing bugs, writing/updating tests, and maintaining project documentation. The developer directed the work, reviewed outputs and tested the deployed product.

### What AI does not do inside the product
- No LLM generates tuition, salary, exchange-rate, loan or ROI numbers at runtime.
- No AI model predicts an individual's admission, job, visa or permanent-residency outcome.
- The financial engine uses TypeScript functions for EMI, cash-flow simulation, NPV, IRR, payback and ROI.
- The recommendation engine uses explicit rules and editable weights rather than an opaque generated answer.
- AI-assisted research is not treated as a substitute for source verification; research values are stored with source links and evidence-status labels.

**Why this design:** Financial calculations should be reproducible and explainable. Keeping AI in the development workflow rather than asking it to invent numbers at runtime reduces the risk of plausible-sounding but unsupported outputs. Data quality still depends on careful research, review and timely updates.

## 8. Technology stack and architecture

| Layer | Technology | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Framework | Next.js 15 (App Router) | Pages, routing, server-side data loading and deployment structure |
| UI | React 19 | Interactive components and user-facing flows |
| Language | TypeScript 5 | Typed data structures and calculation logic |
| Styling | Tailwind CSS 4 | Responsive layout and styling |
| Charts | Recharts | Visualising financial scenarios and projections |
| Research data | Version-controlled JSON | Country, programme, lender, salary, cost and policy data reviewed in Git |
| Financial engine | Custom TypeScript modules | EMI, monthly loan simulation, NPV, IRR, payback, ROI and scenario modelling |
| FX service | Frankfurter API / ECB reference rates | Latest available FX reference rates, cached for 24 hours |
| Fallback | Local JSON fallback | Keeps app usable if live FX retrieval fails; fallback is labelled not current |
| Profile persistence | Browser localStorage | Keeps profile/assumptions on the user's device; no profile database/login |
| Testing | Vitest | Unit and data-integrity tests |
| Hosting | Vercel | Public deployment and production build |
| Version control | GitHub | Source code, data changes, audit trail and documentation |

**Architecture in one view:**  
Official sources → curated JSON dataset → Next.js/Vercel data loading → React UI + TypeScript finance/recommendation functions → comparisons, scenarios and source links.

Student profile and assumptions are stored in browser localStorage. Research data lives in the repository and is served to the app. The MVP has no database or server-side user accounts. The data model could later be migrated to PostgreSQL if the product grows.

## 9. Testing and quality controls

The repository includes automated tests for the financial engine and data integrity. The README documents `npm test` and `npm run build` as key verification commands. The data audit records source checks and corrections made on 22 October 2026.

| Test area | What should be verified |
|---|---|
| Financial functions | EMI, loan balance, processing fee, funding gap, moratorium interest, NPV, IRR and payback behaviour |
| Data integrity | Programme records pass through the model; missing/unverified values are not silently presented as verified facts |
| Inputs and scenarios | Changing loan, savings, salary, interest rate, duration or risk preferences updates outputs consistently |
| UX/accessibility | Assessment completion, filters, navigation, mobile layout, labels and missing-data/error states |
| Regression check | Total cost, fee adjustments, loan fee and available funds reconcile to the displayed funding gap |

A live deployment review is not an independent QA certification. Before a high-stakes personal decision, users should verify material fees and policy details directly with the university, lender and relevant government authority.

## 10. Limitations and next steps

### Known limitations
- The programme catalogue is curated and not exhaustive.
- Some records are partly verified; certain mandatory fees or salary details remain incomplete.
- Tax and salary modelling uses simplifying assumptions.
- No accounts, payment flow, institutional portal or cloud-saved profile exists in this MVP.
- The model presents scenarios but does not assign probabilities to job, visa or immigration outcomes.
- The business model is intentionally outside this document and will be developed as a separate next phase.

### Recommended next steps
1. Run 10 observed sessions with students and parents; record each person's shortlist before and after using the app.
2. Measure whether users understand funding gap, EMI and downside scenarios without help.
3. Reconcile all cost, fee and loan figures across the app and add regression tests for each discovered issue.
4. Refresh data only when sources and academic years can be checked; show last-verified dates clearly.
5. Develop and validate the business model after the user problem and product value have been tested.
